# Waypoint sales playbook (knowledge base for drafting)

Condensed in our own words from widely recommended B2B sales books. The draft and judge prompts load this page (Notion › Sales Playbook) on every run. Edit it there; the workflow picks up changes automatically.

## Sources and what we take from each
- **SPIN Selling (Neil Rackham):** big deals move on the buyer's problems and their consequences, not on features. Ask about the situation and the cost of the problem.
- **Gap Selling (Keenan):** sell the gap between where they are now and where they want to be. Name a plausible current state and a desired future state.
- **The Challenger Sale (Dixon & Adamson):** the best sellers teach something useful, tailor it to the person, and stay confident. Lead with an insight, not with yourself.
- **Predictable Revenue (Aaron Ross):** first touches are short, plain and aimed at relevance; the goal is a reply, not a sale.
- **Fanatical Prospecting (Jeb Blount):** relevance beats volume; persistence across channels wins, politely.
- **Never Split the Difference (Chris Voss):** "no-oriented" questions lower pressure ("Would it be crazy to…?", "Is it a bad idea to…?"); labels show you understood ("It sounds like…").
- **Influence (Robert Cialdini):** reciprocity (give something first), authority (one credible proof point), liking (be human), never fake scarcity.
- **To Sell Is Human (Daniel Pink):** attunement (see it from their side), clarity (help them see their problem in a new way).
- **The Sales Development Playbook (Trish Bertuzzi):** multi-touch, multi-channel sequences with a clear end; every touch adds something new.
- **Founding Sales (Pete Kazanjian):** early-stage buyers buy from people who understand their stage; credibility comes from specifics.

## Rules for a first message
1. **Their world first.** Open with their situation or goal (what they build, where they want to go), not with Noah and not with the funding round.
2. **The round is context, not the headline.** Mention it at most once, in half a sentence ("now that the round is closed…"). Never congratulate at length, never put it in the subject.
3. **One plausible problem or gap.** What typically gets hard at this stage (finding partners in a new market, first commercial hire, turning pilots into a repeatable motion). Phrase it as a likely challenge, never as a claim about them.
4. **"How can we help" framing.** Offer a concrete outcome they would want, in their words. No feature list, no price, no "services".
5. **One proof point.** Exactly one, from Noah's profile, the one closest to their gap.
6. **Low-pressure ask.** One question that is easy to answer, ideally no-oriented ("Would it be a bad idea to compare notes for 20 minutes?").
7. **Short.** Email 60–110 words. LinkedIn note under 280 characters. Short sentences, no paragraphs longer than three lines.
8. **Plain language.** No hype, no buzzwords, no fake urgency, no "I hope this email finds you well", no "quick question" subjects.
9. **Human and specific.** It must be obvious the message could only have been written to this company.
10. **Easy exit.** End emails with a polite opt-out line.

## Three approaches (the draft writes one of each; Noah picks)
- **A · Problem first (SPIN / Gap):** their goal → the likely gap at this stage → how Noah closes it → one proof → question.
- **B · Insight first (Challenger):** one useful observation about their market or stage → what that means for them → offer to share more → question.
- **C · Short and direct (Predictable Revenue / Voss):** three or four sentences, one proof point, a no-oriented question.

## Language and tone
- **German:** "du", greeting "Hi <Vorname>,", natural startup tone, no stiff phrases ("Sehr geehrte", "Mit freundlichen Grüßen"), keep English terms only where Germans use them (Go-to-Market, Pipeline).
- **English:** "Hi <first name>,", warm and direct, British/European rather than US hype.
- Subject lines: 3–7 words, about their goal or market, lower case allowed, never clickbait.

## Follow-up principles (for the follow-up engine, built later)
- Every follow-up adds something new (an insight, a relevant example, a different angle); never "just bumping this".
- Stop immediately on any reply, opt-out or bounce.
- Mix channels (email, LinkedIn) rather than repeating the same email.
- Draft cadence to discuss: day 0 email · day 3 LinkedIn connect · day 7 email (insight) · day 12 email (short question) · day 18 break-up email. Each send needs approval.

## Anti-patterns (the judge rejects these)
- Opening with "Congratulations on your raise!" or any flattery.
- Talking about Noah before the recipient.
- Claims about their problems stated as facts ("You are struggling with…").
- More than one proof point, any invented number, any price.
- Generic lines that would fit any startup.
